A Metacase in society

A cold-chain warehouse operator has run six weeks of exception-handling training for frontline supervisors at three hubs. A fourth hub, deferred for scheduling reasons, kept its operating records for the same weeks as a reference. A joint acceptance meeting on 8 June will decide on three work packages the provider has claimed, 816,000 yuan in all; the joint lead for learning effectiveness and procurement acceptance must have recommendations ready, and the evidence closes on 31 May. All entities and data are synthetic.

The difficulty is that none of the 17 source files is fully settled. The authorisation list includes one person in the wrong role, three attendance records lack a sign-out, two supervisors have both a practice and a formal attempt at the delayed test, six pairs of observers disagree, and the end of one chiller repair is recorded a day apart. How each is handled bears directly on a package's status and amount. The provider, citing “materials uploaded”, rates all three packages as accepted.

The meeting has three questions to answer at once. Operations asks whether incident handling improved. Procurement and finance ask which contract thresholds each package met and how much to recommend. Hub managers ask whether a shortfall reflects what supervisors learned or the chances they had to use it. Personal learning data may serve evaluation, coaching and acceptance, not discipline. The agent must hand in a workbook that can be recomputed and a decision memo; it may recommend, not approve. Restating a pending item as verified leaves a recommended amount without a basis, and personal data used beyond evaluation breach the data-use rule.

01Context & evidence

Scoping the evidence and its status; three packages to decide.

The role is joint lead for learning effectiveness and procurement acceptance. Training ended on 15 March and ten weeks of on-the-job observation on 24 May; evidence dated after the cutoff can only be listed for later closure. The materials use several statuses (pending verification, pending a spot check, pending review, estimated, awaiting approval), and the task requires each to be kept as it stands.

  1. EvidenceEight detail files, five workbooks and four documents, including 4,940 test responses, 755 attendance events, 341 incident-opportunity records, 1,145 behaviour observations, 720 incident tickets and 96 hub-week operating records.
  2. Open itemsAn acceptance status and a recommended amount for each package: A at 244,800 yuan; B at 367,200 yuan in four equal evidence units; C at 204,000 yuan in two equal validation units.
  3. ConstraintVersion 1.1 of the settlement rules states that “uploaded” is not “reviewed”. Items pending, estimated or awaiting approval keep that status, and the role cannot approve payment.
  4. Data boundaryPersonal learning data may serve evaluation, coaching and acceptance; they may not support discipline, reassignment, pay cuts or dismissal.

02Prior practice & strategy

Choosing measures by contract threshold; separating opportunity from ability.

The contract sets two thresholds for each package. The materials also supply the amount rules and the pre-review meeting's provisional view; the agent's choice of measures is kept only in its step record.

  1. Thresholds for ADelivery of the training units, verified; and effective learning time of at least 85% of the planned 1,620 minutes, met by at least 90% of supervisors.
  2. Thresholds for B and CB requires delayed-test coverage of at least 90%, and behaviour attainment of at least 75% among supervisors with two or more verified incidents. C requires a positive net improvement over the reference hub on at least two core metrics, and a close-out with a usable index and no high-risk open item marked as closed.
  3. Candidate treatmentsThe provider claims full acceptance of all three packages. The pre-review meeting of 29 May leans towards conditional acceptance of A and partial acceptance of B and C by evidence unit. After recomputing each threshold, the agent adopts the latter and splits the amounts by unit.
  4. Choice of measureBehaviour attainment can be computed three ways, and the agent computes all of them: on the mean of all reviewed items, all 73 supervisors attain it; on the mean of the last round, 67 do; requiring all five ratings in the last round to reach 3, 53 do. It adopts the second.
  5. Comparison designThe deferred fourth hub serves as the same-period reference; the net change is the difference between each trained hub's change from baseline to observation and the reference hub's. The deferred hub had not yet been trained, so it nets out shocks common to all hubs.

03Actions & execution

Reading and recomputing each source, then building two files.

gemini-3.8-flash runs in the DeepSeek Harness for about 19 minutes: 86 model requests and 85 tool calls, all logged.

  1. Evidence gatheringThe agent reads the four documents, then each sheet of the five workbooks and the eight detail files, checking the status fields for attendance, tests, incidents, observations and disruptions. It begins generating deliverables about seven minutes in.
  2. RecomputationEffective learning time reaches the 1,377-minute threshold for 82 of 83 supervisors; one stands at 1,170 minutes. Three supervisors each have 360 minutes pending verification; these are held out, leaving each at 1,440 minutes. Mean test scores at the three points are 59.06, 94.07 and 90.27.
  3. StratificationWith two verified incidents as the threshold, 73 supervisors can be evaluated, 8 lack opportunity, and 2 are restricted by leave or transfer.
  4. FailureThe script that builds the memo halts on taking the first row of an empty table. The agent identifies the cause, rewrites the script and regenerates the file.
  5. Read-backIt reads both files back once, checking sheet counts, dimensions and key figures. The workbook has ten sheets; three cells hold formulas and the rest typed values.

04Outcomes & verification

Out-of-loop verification: 34 criteria, judged singly.

Harbor adds the rubric to the same container only after the agent stops. RewardKit runs one session per criterion on another model; nothing flows back.

  1. Acceptance decisionsThe status and every amount of all three packages are judged correct. A is accepted conditionally: 220,320 yuan for approval, 24,480 retained. B passes three of four units: 275,400 for approval, 91,800 deferred. C passes one of two: 102,000 for approval, 102,000 deferred.
  2. A measure at odds with the rubricBehaviour attainment is reported as 67 of 73. The rubric sets the measure as a mean of at least 3.0 over all reviewed behaviour items, which gives 73 of 73. Both exceed the 75% contract threshold, so the decision on B is unaffected.
  3. Not shown directlyEach trained hub's change net of the deferred hub is not listed; only a pooled figure appears, such as 4.33 percentage points on the response-SLA rate. By hub it should read 6.5, 4.5 and 2.0. The figures needed are in the sheet; what is missing is one subtraction and one line showing the result.
  4. Attribution and traceabilityThe memo says the data “irrefutably prove” the training's independent value and does not limit the link between coaching and behaviour to a descriptive association. The workbook has no source index, so conclusions cannot be traced to source files by key.
  5. Score structureOf 28 positive criteria, 19 met and 9 not met; none of six penalties triggered. 79 of 106: one pilot run's score, not accuracy or a ranking.

05Reflection & reuse

Reviewing every disposition, and the limits of reuse.

The record ties each loss to what the agent saw and did, so a later attempt starts from a specific failure rather than a score.

  1. What heldThe ten criteria on scope, attendance and tests are met, as are three on opportunity strata, observation records and keeping status: 13 in all. The status and amounts of all three packages are correct, and no penalty was triggered.
  2. Supervisor scope and shift roster
    What it shows
    The authorisation list has 84 names; one is a warehouse coordinator, marked as listed in error.
    Why it matters
    Scope precedes every rate; one extra person in the denominator dilutes each rate that follows.
    This run
    Excluded; 83 in scope.
  3. Attendance, make-up and exemption events
    What it shows
    Three supervisors each have 360 minutes of attendance without a sign-out, pending verification.
    Why it matters
    Pending records cannot count as confirmed learning time, nor be restated as verified; restating them would carry unverified time straight into the basis for payment.
    This run
    Held out; all three still exceed the 1,377-minute threshold. Because the three sign-outs remain unverified, package A is accepted conditionally.
  4. Three-point situational test responses
    What it shows
    Two supervisors each made a practice attempt, marked void, the day before the delayed test. The provider calls the item closed; the internal status reverted to pending a spot check on 28 May.
    Why it matters
    Coverage and scores rest on formal, valid attempts only; counting practice attempts would change both.
    This run
    Practice attempts excluded; 79 valid delayed tests.
  5. Incident opportunity log
    What it shows
    Ten supervisors have one verified incident; two of them are restricted by leave or transfer.
    Why it matters
    No opportunity is not no ability. The data-use rules forbid recording a lack of opportunity as zero or as failure.
    This run
    73 evaluable, 8 lacking opportunity, 2 restricted; the penalty for marking them as failing not triggered.
  6. On-site behaviour coding
    What it shows
    Observers disagree on 18 of 1,145 observations, all on one behaviour, for six supervisors.
    Why it matters
    Until the disagreement is resolved, the evidence unit concerned cannot be treated as passed; the amount rules say that a unit whose key source is pending does not pass.
    This run
    Kept pending; B's behaviour-transfer unit deferred.
  7. Weekly hub operating metrics
    What they show
    Figures for the week of 18 May are estimates at all four hubs.
    Why it matters
    An estimate is not a measurement; keeping or dropping that week changes the net change that is read off.
    This run
    Kept as estimates, with a recomputation excluding that week.
  8. Equipment, weather and cargo disruptions
    What they show
    The end of one chiller repair is recorded a day apart. The pre-review meeting notes that one hub's improvement overlaps with a chiller repair.
    Why it matters
    A one-day gap in the repair's end decides whether an improvement falls before or after the repair, and with it the attribution; net improvement cannot all be attributed to training. The rubric asks for a recheck at plus or minus one day and for separate treatment of extreme-heat weeks, sensitive cargo and a handheld-terminal upgrade.
    This run
    The item is placed at SYN-HUB-01 where the rubric names SYN-HUB-03; none of the four treatments is completed.
  9. What failedAnalysis and argument earned 0 of 6 points and the hub-level net changes 0 of 9. The figures were computed correctly but not laid out for a decision-maker to read, and the limits of the conclusion were not stated. These losses are not arithmetic, and a script's self-check would not find them.
