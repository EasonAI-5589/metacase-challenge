econ-0157 · one recorded pilot run

A Metacase in economics

From 2013, central inspection teams entered China's government departments in successive waves. Did an inspection change how many candidates competed for each civil-service post? Lai and Li (The Journal of Law and Economics, 2024) answer with a panel of 717 recruiting departments over 2012–2017. The task is to reproduce one estimate from that paper, Table 2, column 1: a coefficient, its standard error and its p-value.

The difficulty is that inspections arrived in five cohorts: an ordinary two-way fixed-effects regression would use departments already inspected as controls for those inspected later, so differences in timing enter the estimate. The paper instead uses the Sun–Abraham interaction-weighted estimator with never-inspected departments as the control cohort. The specification leaves a second gap: the task names the authors' Stata script but does not supply it, and that script estimates the same effect twice, without covariates and with five.

The container has Python, no Stata, and open network access. The agent must find the original specification, rebuild the estimator in Python and settle on one of the two versions. A deterministic grader compares three numbers: the coefficient and the standard error must be within 1% of the reference, and the p-value within 0.01. Both specifications yield a result; reporting column 2 would put the coefficient 4.6% off, outside the tolerance, and score 0.

01Context & evidence

Scoping the data and the estimand, and what the task leaves open.

The data are one Stata file. Before estimating anything, the agent has to establish which estimate the data support, which settings the task fixes and which it leaves out.

  1. Data3,246 department-year observations on 717 departments over six years, with 30 labelled variables.
  2. VariablesThe outcome comp is the applicant-to-hire ratio (labelled AHR); inspectmain marks a leading department that has been inspected. Three variables are marked for the Sun–Abraham estimator.
  3. Treatment and sampleOf the 717 departments, 119 are leading departments, and the treatment variable equals 1 only among them; 87 were inspected between 2013 and 2017, and 180 observations are treated.
  4. Open itemsOne coefficient with its standard error and p-value, and a standalone Python script that reproduces them.
  5. ConstraintThe task fixes four settings: department and year fixed effects, cohorts by first inspection year, never-inspected departments as controls, and clustering by department. There is no Stata, and the agent has 30 minutes.

02Prior practice & strategy

Returning to the authors' code for the specification, then choosing the column.

Prior practice is the paper's public replication package; the agent's choice is kept only in its step record and final statement.

  1. Rules and practiceAt 103 seconds the agent downloads the package from the author's page and reads the script the task names. Table 2 comes from eventstudyinteract: cohort-specific effects are estimated first, then weighted by each cohort's share of treated observations.
  2. A path easily confusedIn the script, plain two-way fixed effects belong to an appendix table, not to Table 2.
  3. Candidate specificationsColumn 1 has no covariates. Column 2 adds five: two shares of posts without eligibility restrictions, two shares by education requirement, and log GDP per capita.
  4. Trade-offThe agent computes both columns, −66.33 and −69.35, and searches the web for the task's phrase “row-specific covariates per table column”. It reports column 1: the four settings the task lists, with no covariates.
  5. Limit of the recordThis run kept no reasoning text. The reason for choosing column 1 appears only in the final statement: “without additional covariates”.

03Actions & execution

Rebuilding the estimator from Stata source; reconciling each convention.

gemini-3.8-flash runs in the Codex harness for about 13 minutes: 103 model requests and 102 tool calls, all logged.

  1. Source readingThe agent downloads the source of eventstudyinteract, avar, ivreg2 and reghdfe and reads how each estimates weights, clustered variance and degrees-of-freedom corrections.
  2. Cohort weightsThe five cohorts hold 14, 8, 65, 79 and 14 treated observations, giving weights of 0.078, 0.044, 0.361, 0.439 and 0.078. Cohort effects of −38.2, +27.6, −49.2, −104.9 and −9.7 combine to −66.33.
  3. Sample definitionThe library pyhdfe, used to remove fixed effects, returns 3,170 rows where the data hold 3,246. The agent finds 76 departments observed only once (singletons); Stata's reghdfe drops them by default. It applies the same setting, and the clusters fall from 717 to 641.
  4. Two parts of the varianceThe variance of the combined effect adds two terms: one for the cohort effects, which alone gives a standard error of 24.890, and one for estimating the cohort weights. Together they give 25.053.
  5. FailuresA self-check halts on a NameError and passes once corrected. The harness rejects a command to delete temporary files, and the agent leaves them in place.

04Outcomes & verification

Out-of-loop verification: a grader compares three numbers.

Harbor adds the grader and the reference to the container only after the agent stops. The grader calls no model; it reads the result file and compares it with the reference.

  1. Verified figuresA coefficient of −66.3269 and a standard error of 25.0531. Compared at the full precision of the result file, the coefficient matches the reference to 12 decimal places and the standard error to seven.
  2. Basis of the p-valueThe agent uses a t distribution with 640 degrees of freedom and reports 0.00831; the reference uses the normal distribution, 0.00811. The gap of 0.0002 lies within the tolerance of 0.01.
  3. Decision ruleA relative error of at most 1% in the coefficient and in the standard error, and an absolute error of at most 0.01 in the p-value, score 1. Anything else scores 0; there is no partial credit.
  4. The weight of a specificationThe agent's result for column 2 is −69.35 with a standard error of 22.67. Reported instead, it would be 4.6% off in the coefficient and 9.5% in the standard error, and would score 0 under the same rule.

05Reflection & reuse

Reviewing the choice of specification, and the limits of a replication.

The record breaks one number into the settings it depends on, so a later attempt can check each of them rather than compare results.

  1. What heldThe estimator, control cohort, fixed effects, clustering and treatment of singletons all match the source code, and the script reproduces the result when run alone.
  2. Variable labels in the data
    What they show
    Three variables, including never_inspect, are marked for the Sun–Abraham estimator.
    Why it matters
    When treatment arrives in waves, units not yet treated will be treated later and cannot serve as controls throughout; with the wrong controls, differences in timing between cohorts are read as treatment effects.
    This run
    never_inspect used as the control cohort.
  3. The authors' script, Table 2
    What it shows
    Columns 1 and 2 differ only in whether five covariates enter.
    Why it matters
    With the same estimator and the same data, that choice alone moves the coefficient by 3.02 and the standard error by 2.38.
    This run
    Both computed; column 1 reported.
  4. Source of eventstudyinteract
    What it shows
    The variance of the combined effect has two terms, for the cohort effects and for the cohort weights.
    Why it matters
    The first term alone gives a standard error of 24.89 rather than 25.05: the variance is understated, the confidence interval too narrow and the t statistic too large.
    This run
    Both terms included.
  5. Default setting of reghdfe
    What it shows
    The 76 departments observed once are dropped from the sample by default.
    Why it matters
    Dropping them sets the sample (3,246 or 3,170), the clusters (717 or 641) and the degrees-of-freedom correction; a change in any of the three moves the standard error and the p-value.
    This run
    Dropped, as in the source.
  6. How the result is returned
    What it shows
    The authors' script returns its estimates without degrees of freedom; the reference p-value agrees with the normal distribution.
    Why it matters
    For the same t statistic of −2.647, the two bases differ by 0.0002. A p-value depends on the assumed distribution and its degrees of freedom, so a report should state its basis.
    This run
    The agent also computed the normal-basis value, 0.00811, which matches the reference, but reported the t-based one; the gap is within tolerance.
  7. What failedThe basis of the p-value differs from the reference and passes only on tolerance. The reason for choosing column 1 was not recorded, so that step cannot be reviewed. Temporary files were left in place. The self-check caught a coding error; it could not catch a choice of specification or of basis.
  8. Conditions and signatureApplies where source code defines the estimator and the task does not state the full specification. Signature: open specification, source comparison, numerical replication.

Conditions and signature

What carries beyond this case, and where the evidence stops
DimensionWhat this case showsLimits of the evidence
Real-world depthThe question comes from a published paper: treatment in five cohorts, never-treated units as controls, clustering by department. Reproducing it takes more than a regression command: cohort weights, a two-part variance, dropped singletons and the basis of the p-value. In this pilot the covariates alone moved the coefficient by 4.6%, outside the tolerance.Data and specification come from the authors' public replication package. The task reproduces one coefficient; the paper's event-study figures, mechanisms and robustness checks are outside it.
GeneralisabilityThe same task package, container and grader apply to any Model + Harness pair. Other tasks in the econometric replication subtrack use the same comparison of coefficient, standard error and p-value.This page shows one run. Whether the same pair gives a stable result on repeated runs has not been tested.
Path to practiceThe deliverables are what a research assistant hands over: the numbers and a script that reproduces them. Computing every candidate specification before choosing, recording the reason for the choice, checking corrections against source code and reconciling the sample size can become a checklist for replication work.A full score shows that this number was reproduced, not that the script is correct for other data or specifications. The basis of the p-value still differs from the reference.
Research relevanceOne public script yields two specifications with the same sign and significance, yet the coefficients differ by 4.6%, enough to put a result outside the tolerance. The step record breaks one number into the settings it depends on, so a deviation in a replication can be traced to a specific choice; the choice of specification and the basis of the p-value are what a script's self-check cannot catch. Our ModularRSI study raised strict replication from 64% to 67% on tasks of this kind.The task does not name the target column, a gap in the task text, and the reason for choosing column 1 was not recorded. The agent had network access and obtained the authors' public code: the record shows replication from published materials, not an independent derivation. This run did not use ModularRSI.

Grader detail: relative error 0.00% in the coefficient and in the standard error; absolute error 0.0002 in the p-value.

External addresses are counted by the URLs appearing in commands: the author's page and replication package, the Boston College archive of Stata programs, and GitHub. Only the response headers of the journal's page were requested; the text of the paper was not read.
